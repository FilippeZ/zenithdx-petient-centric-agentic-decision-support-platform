### **Alzheimer’s Disease, Symptoms, Diagnosis, Treatment**

#### **What is Alzheimer’s Disease?**
Alzheimer’s disease is the most common **cause of dementia**, a condition that affects memory, thinking, and daily functioning. It happens due to the **buildup of abnormal proteins** in the brain, forming **amyloid plaques** and **neurofibrillary tangles**. These protein accumulations damage brain cells, leading to their gradual death and brain shrinkage.

Currently, over **6.9 million people** aged 65+ in the U.S. live with Alzheimer’s, and worldwide, around **60%-70% of people with dementia** have this disease.

Alzheimer’s **progresses over time**, causing memory loss and cognitive decline that eventually interferes with daily life. There is **no cure**, but treatments may **slow the disease** and improve symptoms.

---

### **Symptoms**
Alzheimer’s affects the brain in many ways. Early on, people **forget recent events or conversations**. Over time, they lose the ability to **think clearly, make decisions, and perform daily tasks**.

#### **Key Symptoms:**
1. **Memory loss:** Forgetting recent conversations, misplacing objects, or struggling to recognize familiar faces.
2. **Difficulty thinking and reasoning:** Problems with numbers, managing finances, or multitasking.
3. **Poor judgment:** Making inappropriate decisions or being unaware of safety risks.
4. **Struggles with daily activities:** Trouble cooking, dressing, or performing familiar tasks.
5. **Personality and behavior changes:** Increased aggression, depression, anxiety, social withdrawal, or paranoia.

Some skills like **singing, storytelling, or drawing** may remain intact for longer because they are controlled by areas of the brain affected later in the disease.

---

### **Causes of Alzheimer’s**
The **main cause** of Alzheimer’s is **abnormal protein deposits in the brain**:
- **Amyloid plaques** form between neurons and disrupt communication.
- **Tau tangles** develop inside brain cells, leading to their dysfunction and death.

Over time, these changes **shrink brain tissue** and affect **memory, reasoning, and movement**.

#### **Risk Factors:**
- **Age:** Most cases occur in people over 65.
- **Genetics:** Certain genes, such as **APOE ε4**, increase risk.
- **Heart conditions:** High blood pressure, diabetes, and high cholesterol raise the likelihood of developing Alzheimer’s.
- **Lifestyle factors:** Smoking, obesity, and lack of exercise contribute to risk.
- **Brain injuries:** Traumatic brain injuries increase the risk.

---

### **Diagnosis**
Alzheimer’s is diagnosed through a combination of **symptom assessments, cognitive tests, and brain imaging**.

#### **Key Diagnostic Tests:**
1. **Memory and cognitive tests** – Assess memory, problem-solving, and reasoning skills.
2. **MRI or CT scans** – Look for brain shrinkage or structural abnormalities.
3. **PET scans** – Detect amyloid plaques and tau tangles.
4. **Blood tests** – Identify Alzheimer’s-related proteins and rule out other conditions.
5. **Genetic testing** – Helps determine if a person has a high risk due to family history.

Advances in **biomarker tests** now allow early detection by measuring **amyloid and tau** in the **blood or cerebrospinal fluid**.

---

### **Treatment Options**
There is **no cure** for Alzheimer’s, but some treatments **slow its progression** and manage symptoms.

#### **Medications for Symptom Management:**
1. **Cholinesterase inhibitors** (Donepezil, Rivastigmine, Galantamine) – Improve memory and thinking by increasing brain chemicals that support nerve communication.
2. **Memantine (NMDA antagonist)** – Helps protect brain cells by controlling **glutamate**, a chemical that contributes to nerve damage.
3. **Newer treatments** (Lecanemab, Donanemab) – Monoclonal antibodies that **reduce amyloid plaques**, slowing disease progression in early Alzheimer’s.

#### **Other Supportive Treatments:**
- **Antidepressants** – Help with mood changes and anxiety.
- **Antipsychotics** – Manage paranoia, hallucinations, and aggression.
- **Therapy and cognitive exercises** – Help maintain daily functioning and mental health.

**Lifestyle modifications**, such as a **healthy diet, exercise, and social engagement**, can also help slow cognitive decline.

---

### **Prevention & Outlook**
Alzheimer’s **cannot be completely prevented**, but certain **lifestyle choices** may lower the risk:
- Eating a **Mediterranean diet** rich in fruits, vegetables, and healthy fats.
- **Exercising regularly** to improve brain health.
- Staying **mentally active** through reading, puzzles, or learning new skills.
- Maintaining **social connections** to reduce cognitive decline.

#### **Life Expectancy**
People with Alzheimer’s **live an average of 4 to 8 years** after diagnosis, though some can live **10+ years** with proper care.

As Alzheimer’s progresses, **it affects basic body functions**, leading to complications like **infections, pneumonia, and organ failure**, which may result in death.

---

### **Final Thoughts**
Alzheimer’s disease is a **progressive brain disorder** that primarily affects **memory, reasoning, and behavior**. It is caused by the **buildup of harmful proteins** that destroy neurons over time. Although there is **no cure**, **medications and lifestyle interventions** can help slow the disease and improve quality of life.
