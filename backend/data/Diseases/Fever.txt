### **Fever: Causes, Symptoms, and Treatment**

---

### **What is a Fever?**
A **fever** is a temporary **increase in body temperature**, usually as a response to an infection. It signals that the body's **immune system** is fighting off harmful invaders, such as bacteria or viruses.

For **most adults and children**, a fever **isn't dangerous**, though it can be uncomfortable. However, in **infants**, even a mild fever **can indicate a serious infection** and requires medical attention.

---

### **What is Considered a Fever?**
Body temperature can vary slightly based on **age, time of day, and activity level**. The **normal body temperature** is about **98.6°F (37°C)**, but a fever is usually defined as:

✔ **100.4°F (38°C) or higher** (oral or rectal temperature)  
✔ **Low-grade fever:** 99.5°F – 100.3°F (37.5°C – 37.9°C)  

🌡️ **Fever readings vary by measurement method:**
- **Rectal and ear thermometers** measure about **1°F (0.6°C) higher** than oral readings.
- **Forehead and armpit thermometers** may show slightly lower readings.

---

### **Common Symptoms of a Fever**
A fever **may come with other symptoms**, depending on the cause:

✔ **Sweating and chills**  
✔ **Shivering or shaking**  
✔ **Headache and muscle aches**  
✔ **Loss of appetite**  
✔ **Fatigue and weakness**  
✔ **Irritability (especially in children)**  
✔ **Dehydration** (dry skin, thirst, dark urine)  

---

### **When to See a Doctor**
Fever is usually **harmless**, but **seek medical attention if:**

#### **For Infants and Toddlers:**
🚨 Call a doctor if your baby is:
✔ **Under 3 months** and has a rectal temp **100.4°F (38°C) or higher**.  
✔ **3-6 months** with a temp **above 102°F (38.9°C)** or seems **sluggish or irritable**.  
✔ **7-24 months** with a fever **above 102°F** lasting more than **one day**.  

#### **For Children:**
🚨 Seek help if your child:
✔ **Has a fever over 102°F (38.9°C)** for more than **three days**.  
✔ **Has a seizure** due to fever.  
✔ **Appears confused, very weak, or has trouble waking up**.  
✔ **Vomits repeatedly or has severe pain** (headache, stomachache, sore throat).  
✔ **Develops a rash or has difficulty breathing**.  

#### **For Adults:**
🚨 Call a doctor if your fever:
✔ **Reaches 103°F (39.4°C) or higher**.  
✔ **Lasts more than three days**.  
✔ **Comes with severe headache, confusion, or difficulty breathing**.  
✔ **Causes chest pain, persistent vomiting, or pain when urinating**.  
✔ **Is accompanied by a stiff neck and sensitivity to light** (signs of meningitis).  

---

### **Causes of Fever**
Fever occurs when the brain's **hypothalamus** (body’s thermostat) raises the body temperature **to fight off illness**. Common causes include:

#### **🦠 Infections:**
✔ **Viral infections** (flu, colds, COVID-19)  
✔ **Bacterial infections** (strep throat, pneumonia, UTIs)  
✔ **Gastrointestinal infections** (food poisoning, stomach flu)  

#### **⚡ Non-Infectious Causes:**
✔ **Heat exhaustion**  
✔ **Autoimmune diseases** (rheumatoid arthritis, lupus)  
✔ **Cancer (rare, but possible cause)**  
✔ **Reactions to medications** (antibiotics, blood pressure meds)  
✔ **After vaccinations** (DTaP, pneumococcal, COVID-19 vaccines)  

---

### **Complications of Fever**
🔹 **Febrile Seizures (Children 6 months - 5 years)**  
- Fever can trigger **seizures**, which may involve **shaking, eye rolling, or loss of consciousness**.  
- Though alarming, most febrile seizures **don’t cause long-term harm**.  

💡 **If a seizure happens:**
✔ Place your child **on their side** to prevent choking.  
✔ **Do not put anything in their mouth**.  
✔ Seek **emergency help** if the seizure lasts more than **5 minutes**.  

---

### **Diagnosis of Fever**
Doctors may order tests to **identify the cause**:

✔ **Physical exam & medical history**  
✔ **Nasal or throat swabs** (to check for viruses)  
✔ **Blood tests** (to detect bacterial infections or immune issues)  
✔ **Chest X-ray** (if pneumonia is suspected)  
✔ **Urinalysis** (to check for urinary infections)  

🔬 **Fever of Unknown Origin (FUO)**  
If a fever **lasts longer than three weeks** without a clear cause, further medical evaluation may be needed.

---

### **How to Treat a Fever**
💊 **Mild Fevers (below 102°F) don’t always need treatment.** They can help fight infections.  
However, **if the fever is causing discomfort**, consider:

✔ **Drink plenty of fluids** – Water, broth, and electrolyte drinks help prevent dehydration.  
✔ **Rest** – The body heals best with adequate sleep.  
✔ **Dress lightly** – Wear loose, comfortable clothing and use light blankets.  
✔ **Keep the room cool** – Avoid overheating the body.  

#### **🔹 Medications for Fever:**
✔ **Acetaminophen (Tylenol)** – Safe for **adults and children**.  
✔ **Ibuprofen (Advil, Motrin)** – Not recommended for **infants under 6 months**.  
✔ **Aspirin** – **DO NOT give to children** (risk of Reye’s syndrome).  

🚨 **Warning:** Avoid taking too much medication. **High doses of ibuprofen or acetaminophen** can damage the **liver or kidneys**.  

---

### **Prevention: How to Avoid Fevers**
Although fevers **can't always be prevented**, you can reduce your risk by:

✔ **Getting vaccinated** (flu, COVID-19, etc.).  
✔ **Washing hands frequently** to prevent infections.  
✔ **Avoiding close contact** with sick people.  
✔ **Not sharing food, drinks, or utensils**.  
✔ **Covering your mouth when coughing or sneezing**.  

---

### **Fever: What to Expect**
✔ **Mild fevers go away within 3-4 days** without treatment.  
✔ **If the fever persists or worsens**, see a doctor.  
✔ **Infants and people with weak immune systems** should be monitored closely.  
✔ **Prevent dehydration by drinking fluids regularly**.
