Other Pleural Conditions: This category includes various pleural abnormalities, such as pleural thickening or tumors.

The pleura, comprising the visceral and parietal layers, envelops the lungs and lines the thoracic cavity. Various abnormalities can affect the pleura, leading to a range of clinical conditions. Below is an overview of notable pleural abnormalities:​

1. Pleural Thickening:

This condition involves an increase in the thickness of the pleural membranes and can be categorized as follows:​

Diffuse Pleural Thickening: A widespread fibrosis of the pleura that may result from infections, asbestos exposure, or inflammatory processes. It often leads to restrictive lung function and breathlessness. ​

Pleural Plaques: Localized areas of hyalinized collagen, typically arising from asbestos exposure. These plaques are generally asymptomatic and are considered markers of past asbestos exposure. ​

2. Pleural Tumors:

Tumors affecting the pleura can be benign or malignant:​

Malignant Mesothelioma: A rare and aggressive cancer primarily linked to asbestos exposure, affecting the pleural lining and presenting with chest pain, shortness of breath, and pleural effusion. ​

Solitary Fibrous Tumor: A rare neoplasm that can be benign or malignant, originating from the pleura and often discovered incidentally on imaging studies. ​





3. Tumor-Like Pleural Disorders:

These conditions may mimic tumors but are benign:​

Thoracic Splenosis: Implantation of splenic tissue in the thoracic cavity, usually following traumatic injury. It is often asymptomatic and discovered incidentally. ​

Catamenial Pneumothorax: A spontaneous pneumothorax occurring in conjunction with menstruation, often due to thoracic endometriosis. ​
